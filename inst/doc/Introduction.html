## Loading required package: SA25204132

Zhang et al.(2024) proposed a five-step algorithm for clustering high-dimensional time series as followings. See more in https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2023.2183132.

Step 1 (Estimation for the number of factors.) For \(0 \leq k \leq k_0\), let \(\hat{\lambda}_{k,1} \geq \cdots \geq \hat{\lambda}_{k,p} \geq 0\) be the eigenvalues of matrix \(\hat\Sigma_{y}(k)\hat\Sigma_{y}(k)^T.\) For a pre-specified positive integer \(J_0 \leq p\), put\(\hat{R}_0 = 1\) and

\[\hat{R}_j = \sum_{k=0}^{k_0} \hat{\lambda}_{k,j} / \sum_{k=0}^{k_0} \hat{\lambda}_{k,j+1}, \quad 1 \leq j \leq J_0. \]

We say that \(\hat{R}_s\) attains a local maximum if \(\hat{R}_s > \max\{\hat{R}_{s-1}, \hat{R}_{s+1}\}\). Let \(\hat{R}_{\hat{\tau}_1}\) and \(\hat{R}_{\hat{\tau}_2}\) be the two largest local maximums among \(\hat{R}_1, \ldots, \hat{R}_{J_0-1}\). The estimators for the numbers of factors are then defined as

\[ \hat{r}_0 = \min\{\hat{\tau}_1, \hat{\tau}_2\}, \quad \hat{r}_0 + \hat{r} = \max\{\hat{\tau}_1, \hat{\tau}_2\}. \]

Step 2 (Estimation for the loadings for common factors.) Let \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{p}\) be the orthonormal eigenvectors of matrix \(\hat{\mathbf{M}}\), arranged according to the descending order of the corresponding eigenvalues. The estimated loading matrix for the common factors is

\[\hat{\mathbf{A}} = (\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\hat r_0}).\]

Step 3 (Estimation for the loadings for cluster-specific factors.) Replace \(\mathbf{y}_{t}\) by \((\mathbf{I}_{p} - \hat{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})\mathbf{y}_{t}\) in (11), and repeat the eigenanalysis as in Step 2 above but now denote the corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors by \(\hat{\mathbf{\xi}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{\xi}}_{p}\). The estimated loading matrix for the cluster-specific factors is

\[\hat{\mathbf{B}} = (\hat{\mathbf{\xi}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{\xi}}_{\hat r}).\]

Step 4 (Identification for the components not belonging to any clusters.) Let \(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{b}}_{p}\) denote the row vectors of \(\mathbf{B}\). Then the identified index set for the components of \(\mathbf{y}_{t}\) not belonging to any clusters is

\[\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{d+1} = \{j : 1 \leq j \leq p, \|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{j}\| \leq \omega_{p}\},\]

where \(\omega_{p} > 0\) is a constant satisfying the conditions \(\omega_{p} = o(p^{\delta/2-1/2}), p^{-1/2}\omega_{p}^{-1} = o(1)\) and \(\frac{p^{\delta}n^{-1}r + p^{-\delta}}{p^{1-\delta}\omega_{p}^{2}} = o(1)\).

Step 5 (K-means clustering.) Denote by \(\hat{d}\) the number of eigenvalues of \(\|\hat{\mathbf{B}}\hat{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}|\) greater than \(1 - \log^{-1} n\), which is taken as an upper bound of the number of clusters. Let \(\hat{p}_{0} = p - |\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{d+1}|\), and \(\hat{\mathbf{F}}\) be the \(\hat{p}_{0} \times \hat{\mathbf{r}}\) matrix obtained from \(\hat{\mathbf{B}}\) by removing the rows with their indices in \(\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{d+1}\). Let \(\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{1}, \ldots, \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{p_{0}}\) denote the \(\hat{p}_{0}\) rows of \(\hat{\mathbf{F}}\). Let \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) be the \(\hat{p}_{0} \times \hat{p}_{0}\) matrix with the \((\ell, m)\)th element

\[\hat{p}_{\ell,m} = \left| \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\ell}^{\top} \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{m} \right| / \left| \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\ell}^{\top} \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\ell} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{m}^{\top} \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{m} \right|^{1/2}, \quad 1 \leq \ell, m \leq \hat{p}_{0}.\]

Perform the K-means clustering (with \(L^{2}\)-distance) for the \(\hat{p}_{0}\) rows of \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) to form the \(d\) clusters, where \(d \leq \hat{d}\) is chosen such that the within-cluster-sum of \(L^{2}\)-distances (to the cluster center points) are stabilized.

We conduct several functions for this algorithm: for step 1, for step 2 and step 3, for step 4 and step 5. Since it is a time-consuming task to perform step 1-3 on a high-dimensional dataset, we also rebuild the first two functions with Rcpp, called and , respectively.
